Arnie Jordan here, coming to you from row three at Jordan Arena, with the Rinkside Report. Hello to both of my listeners, and to my neighbour, who says he listens in the car. I have never once seen that man drive. It was Sunday, October twenty-fifth, and after last week I bought a bigger notebook, the fancy kind with the little ribbon bookmark. By the end of the night I was writing on the ribbon. Thirty-five goals, if you count the shootout, and I do.

The early game was the Regatta Nordiques against Dawson's Oilers. Whetlam opened the scoring, from Langlois and Roy, and Craigy tied it late in the first, set up by McPherson and, I checked this twice, Dawson. The goalie. A goalie with an assist. That man passes better than half the fellas skating in front of him.

Now, I started a brand new page in the notebook, and at the top I wrote one word: Stuart. Two trips in the first period and a hook in the second. Nine minutes in the box. I believe he's asking the league for his own key and a little mat that says welcome. And Craigy's tying goal came while Stuart was sitting in there. Shorthanded. Craigy, cleaning up after his teammate like a fella with a mop at a wedding. The Nordiques kept Stuart company: Andersen and Pulo for tripping, and Whetlam for a high stick, so Whetlam had a goal and a penalty. The full combo meal. Somewhere in there a dump-in hit the stanchion and came all the way back out to centre. I didn't see who shot it, and the puck isn't talking.

McPherson put the Oilers ahead in the second, and Trigg came in to replace McKay in the Nordiques net. In the third, Pulo tied it and Lawrence put the Nordiques ahead, then McPherson tied it again with forty seconds to go. Overtime solved nothing, so off to a shootout, and Moorzy buried it. Four to three Nordiques. McPherson had two goals and an assist and went home with nothing. That's baking the whole cake and getting handed a cracker.

Game two, the Culp Habs beat the Keg Canucks eight to three. Penney had the hat trick and an assist, Hering and Dixon each had a goal and three helpers, Clark had three assists, and Paul scored twice. Koke, McMillan and Adam Short scored for the Canucks. Halfway through, both teams changed goalies at the exact same second, again. Rennie and Rico out, The Plug and Levar in. Two weeks in a row. It's choreographed. Next week I expect a dance number. Rennie gave up five before the switch and The Plug gave up three after it, so the Canucks got it coming and going. And not one penalty all game. My Stuart page had more ink on it than that entire hockey game.

Then the late game, the ColdBreak Leafs against the Timbit Senators, and friends, sit down. The Senators led seven to three in the second. Obie had two goals and two assists, Simon Murphy had two goals, and Deakin, Holt and Sheridan chipped in. Adams went to the box for tripping, and Holt and Sheridan scored fifty-four seconds apart while he sat there. I took out a pen, a pen, and wrote: Senators in a cakewalk.

The Leafs then scored seven in a row. Parsons got one late in the second, and Haroutunian scored forty-seven seconds into the third. Then Collini scored three goals in one minute and fifty-five seconds. A hat trick in less time than it takes me to climb to row three. Parsons scored again, and Darren Horton finished it all by himself with fifty-one seconds left. Ten to seven Leafs. Horton had five points, Parsons and Pierce had four each, and Schmidt's hooking call was the only thing in the third period the Leafs didn't score on. Hageman, Boomer and Williams had three helpers apiece, which is a lot of passing for a team that lost by three. Seventeen goals, and both goalies went the full sixty. Somebody buy the goal judge a coffee. And Senators, Halloween's next weekend. You got your fright in early.

So that's your week: a shootout, a goalie with an assist, a man with his own page in my notebook, and a four-goal lead that went missing. If anybody finds it, there's a crossed-out cakewalk waiting in row three. If I got your name wrong, or gave your goal to the fella beside you, take it up with the refs, the timekeeper, and how fast you people move out there. Tape your sticks. This has been Arnie Jordan with the Rinkside Report.
