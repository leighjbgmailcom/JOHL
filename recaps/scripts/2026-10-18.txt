Arnie Jordan here, coming to you from row three at Jordan Arena, with the Rinkside Report. Welcome back to both of my listeners. It was Sunday, October eighteenth, and after a week off I showed up with a fresh notebook, two sharpened pencils and a large coffee. I left with half a notebook, one pencil, and a napkin covered in arrows. Thirty goals, three games, and two of them won by a single goal.

The early game was the Regatta Nordiques against the Culp Habs, and Fissel scored forty-five seconds in, set up by Regular. Hunt tied it for the Habs, then Regular scored one himself late in the first, and another in the second, from Brophy and De Bolster. With a name like Regular, you'd expect a regular night. Two goals and an assist? I'd call it premium.

The rest of that second period was a parade to the penalty box. Brophy went for hooking, Paul went for tripping, and De Bolster went for something the timekeeper wrote down so fast I couldn't read it from row three. Two Nordiques in the box in one period, and the Habs didn't score on either one. Somewhere in there a clearing attempt went so high I lost it in the lights. I believe it came down during the second game. I did not see who shot it, and I have decided not to find out.

Paul made up for his trip early in the third, making it three to two, but Moorzee and Fissel answered a minute and a half apart, with Roy setting up both. Hunt got his second, and that's how it ended, five to three Nordiques. Paul had a goal, two assists and a trip to the box, which in this league is what we call a full evening. Trigg went the distance and held the Habs to three.

Game two was the Timbit Senators against the Keg Canucks, and I started a tally of lead changes on my napkin. Davies scored late in the first, and Buynink stopped everything in that period, then handed the net to Shannon. One perfect period, and the rest of the night off.

Then came the second period, and I am still recovering. Seven goals. Williams went to the box for hooking ten seconds in. Ten seconds. I was still blowing on my coffee. Obie made it two nothing, and then the Canucks scored three in a little over three minutes: Pundy, Adam Short, and Leyenhorst. The Senators came back with Sheridan and Davies again, and Leyenhorst got his second to tie it at four. Around goal number six, my first pencil snapped clean in half.

Martin put the Canucks ahead to start the third, with a goal and three assists on the night. That's two leads for the Canucks, and they gave both of them back, which is very generous. Obie tied it, and Schmidt scored the winner all by himself, no assists, didn't ask anybody. Six to five Senators. And then, with forty-seven seconds left and his team down by one, Pundy went to the box for interference. Best seat in the house for the finish, I'll give him that. Better than row three. Murphy had three helpers, and Shannon gave up five and still got the win.

The late game was Dawson's Oilers and the ColdBreak Leafs, and it turned into a hat trick contest. Foran scored fifty-seven seconds in, then Horton and Grossi traded goals all first period. No Pants and Grossi made it four to two Oilers in the second, before Adams and Pierce tied it right back up. Then, halfway through the period, both teams changed goalies at the same moment. Raimondo and Mallet out, Dawson and Rosie in. I thought it was a fire drill. Rosie had been in net for exactly one minute when Grossi scored his third. Welcome to the game.

In the third, Misek went to the box for tripping, and forty-five seconds later Horton finished his own hat trick to tie it at five. Then McPherson scored the winner, unassisted, with five thirty-six left. Last week he had six points. This week he had one, and it was the one that counted. Grossi finished with five points. Horton and Pierce had four each, and the Leafs got nothing for it but a sore neck from watching the scoreboard.

So that's your week: thirty goals, two hat tricks, two six to five finishes, and a napkin with four lead changes on it, every one of them from game two. If I got your name wrong, or gave your goal to the fella beside you, take it up with the refs, the timekeeper, and how fast you people move out there. Tape your sticks, and wave if you see me in row three. This has been Arnie Jordan with the Rinkside Report.
